# The "The" montage

This montage is the last 6 seconds of the video. The word "The" stays on the same pixels while the world behind it cuts from 1776 to today. The last plate is our own "The" in Syne, and "Payments" lands beside it on the downbeat.

## Why it works

- **It's a lineage.** The plates run in date order: the Declaration, then 1912 newspapers, 1920s films, today's mastheads and storefronts, and finally a street in Berkeley. The Payments arrives as the next name in that line, the way the Whop film puts itself after the telegraph and the steam train.
- **It pays off the name.** The brand flips The Social Network's line "Drop the 'The'". The montage picks the "The" back up and shows where it has always stood.
- **The eye never moves.** Every plate is scaled and rotated so its "The" lands on the exact spot where "The" sits in the final "The Payments" lockup. Since that lockup is centred, the anchor is left of frame centre, and "Payments" grows to the right from a fixed left edge.

## The rules for every plate

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Anchor | The capital T. Its cap height, its left edge and the baseline under it match the T of the end card's "The" in Syne to within 2 px. |
| Casing | Title case first. All-caps "THE" also works because the T still aligns, but use it sparingly. Most real signs are all caps. |
| Rotation | Turn each plate so the baseline is level. Correct the perspective on facades shot from below. |
| Grade | Every plate in greyscale with one shared grain. Teal arrives only on the end card, so the product is the first colour the viewer sees. |
| Edges | A plate that doesn't cover the frame after scaling sits on near-black. At 2–4 frames per plate nobody sees the edge. |
| Sound | A receipt printer's chatter that speeds up with the cuts, which ties back to the ticket in beat 1. It stops dead on the last historical plate, and "Payments" lands on the next downbeat. |

## How it's built

1. `plates.json` lists each source with two baseline points for its "The": the left edge of the word and the right edge of the "e". These were read off gridded crops made with `tools/grid.py`.
2. `python3 tools/render.py` maps those points onto the end card's "The". Upright plates get a rotation and scale, and signs shot at an angle (`"mode": "shear"`) get a shear that levels the baseline while keeping stems vertical. Every plate goes to greyscale with moving grain. The script writes the frames, a placeholder click track and `out/the-montage.mp4`.
3. Sources and renders live in `sources/` and `out/`, which git ignores. Re-download them with the URLs in this file.
